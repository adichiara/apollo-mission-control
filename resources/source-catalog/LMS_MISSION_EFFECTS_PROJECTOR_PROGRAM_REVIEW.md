## Primary contextual evidence

NASA SP-4009, *The Apollo Spacecraft: A Chronology*, records that during the week ending 25 August 1967 Grumman proposed a study of a mission effects projector designed and built by Farrand because the item did not meet established specifications. Grumman solicited optomechanical firms; after proposals from Itek, Biorad, Link, and Conductron, Grumman judged Itek's proposal most technically acceptable and proposed a letter contract, in which NASA concurred. The chronology cites the contemporaneous MSC BMR Bethpage weekly report dated 30 August 1967.

Primary NASA copy: https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/sp-4009vol4.pdf

NASA/NTRS record `19680013122`, J. LaRussa, *The Infinity Image System in Visual Simulation*, presented at the AIAA/NASA Manned Space Flight Meeting in November 1964, identifies Farrand's MEP as an image-generation input in its infinity-image visual-simulation technology. The paper describes a non-programmed continuous color film-strip projector for Earth-orbital imagery, moving cloud cover, and sunrise/sunset simulation, with two film cassettes permitting orbital views at different scales.

Primary NTRS copy: https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19680013122/downloads/19680013122.pdf

This 1964 description is a **technology-family baseline only**. It does not prove that the 1967 LMS MEP retained the same film mechanism, cassette arrangement, imagery, control implementation, or requirements.

NASA KSC-130-68 describes mission-simulator peripheral/visual equipment and a mission effects projector supplying Earth and/or lunar scenes according to mission or spacecraft attitude. It is useful system context but is not evidence of a Mission Control station interface or Apollo 13 effectivity.

Primary NASA copy: https://www3.nasa.gov/centers/kennedy/pdf/744319main_1968.pdf

## Primary RG 255 archival route

The NASA/NARA RG 255 preliminary inventory adds two distinct archival routes:

- **E.155B** is explicitly *Reference Files on the Lunar Landing Training Vehicle*, 1962–1973, 20 ft. Its description says there is **also some material relating to** the MEP and EVDE of the Lunar Module mission simulator. This makes E.155B a valid but narrow MEP/EVDE subset target, not an LMS project-file series. Retrieval keys include `Mission Effects Projector`, `MEP`, `External Visual Display Equipment`, `EVDE`, `Lunar Module mission simulator`, old accessions `72A797` and `73ABC1675`, and the inventory locations.
- **E.155B1**, *Project Files on the Lunar Module Simulator, 1968–1970*, is the dedicated LMS series. It includes statements of work, program records, acceptance plans/procedures, technical reports, operations manuals, surface-model photographs, and drawings. The inventory states that most correspondence is with **Kollsman Instrument Corporation** concerning **contract `NAS9-8634` for construction of LM simulators at MSC and KSC**.

Primary inventory: https://www.ibiblio.org/apollo/NARA-SW/Rg255-1.pdf

Archival co-location in E.155B does not establish a technical or organizational relationship between LMS MEP/EVDE and LLTV/LLRV material. Neither series description establishes that Kollsman replaced Farrand, that `NAS9-8634` covered MEP/EVDE, that the 1967 specification problem led to that contract, or that either series represents the Apollo 13 configuration.

## Earlier two-simulator procurement baseline

NASA's *Apollo Spacecraft — A Chronology* and *Astronautics and Aeronautics, 1966* independently record that on **15 February 1966** the converted Grumman LEM development contract required delivery of **two mission simulators**. This predates the 1968–1970 E.155B1 Kollsman / `NAS9-8634` project-file span.

Primary NASA copies:
- https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19800011953/downloads/19800011953.pdf
- https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/1966.pdf

Use: this chronology prevents treating `NAS9-8634` as the origin procurement of the two LMS requirement from the inventory's word “construction” alone. It does **not** define what Kollsman later constructed, modified, replaced, duplicated, integrated, or completed, nor does it establish one-for-one configuration continuity between the 1966 obligation and later MSC/KSC simulators.

## Evidence boundary

Until underlying records are reviewed, do not infer projector signal mappings, the failed specification, units, cadence, precision, acceptance tolerances, corrective actions, vendor succession, station routing, or Apollo 13/1970 effectivity. Do not project the 1964 Farrand film/cassette design forward into the LMS configuration without explicit evidence. Do not infer `NAS9-8634` implementation meaning from chronology alone.
