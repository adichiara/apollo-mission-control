# Research note 240 — LMS handbook digitized-candidate identity gate

Date: 2026-09-17  
Status: **web-accessible handbook candidate located; archival identity not yet proven**

## Question

Can the `67-14186` / `LMA-790-2-LMS` Volume I retrieval target identified in note 239 be recovered as a directly inspectable digitized document without weakening provenance control?

## Primary-source-first result

The primary NASA/MSC bibliographic control remains the authority for archival identity:

- `LMA-790-2-LMS`;
- Grumman Aircraft Engineering Corporation;
- contract `NAS9-1100`;
- MSC accession `67-14186`;
- *Lunar Module Mission Simulator Instructors Handbook, Volume I — Simulator Description*.

A fresh web search located a directly digitized candidate in the Virtual AGC document collection. Its 13 February 2022 collection changelog says it added a document titled **Lunar Module Mission Simulator Instructor's Handbook Volume I, Simulator Description**, describes it as pertaining to the LEM Mission Simulator at the Cape, and says the manual contains switch/display detail and information on how simulator subsystems are simulated.

That is a strong title/content match, but the discovery metadata surfaced in this pass does **not** expose `LMA-790-2-LMS`, accession `67-14186`, `NAS9-1100`, or a title page tying the scan to the MSC catalog record. Therefore the digitized candidate is **not yet promoted as the recovered accession copy**.

## Bibliographic discrepancy discovered

The NASA/MSC corporate-author index and its supplement disagree on the date attached to `67-14186` / `67-14187`:

- the main corporate-author index gives **01-04-67**;
- the supplement gives **15-05-67** for `67-14186` and `67-14187`.

NASA GP-642 (1969), a Kennedy Space Center bibliography, independently lists the handbook as **Apr. 1, 1967**.

Do not silently normalize this discrepancy. It may represent a revision, catalog correction, or metadata error; none is established yet.

## What this closes

The handbook is no longer merely an archival retrieval hypothesis: a public digitized document with the matching title is known to exist.

## What remains unresolved

Before extracting implementation claims from that scan, verify from the scan itself or equivalent provenance metadata:

1. report number / title page;
2. date and revision;
3. Grumman authorship;
4. site applicability (KSC only, MSC/KSC common, or other);
5. relationship to MSC accession `67-14186`.

Until that identity gate is closed, do not use the candidate to assign:

- the original two DDP-224 workloads;
- software/program ownership;
- common-memory mapping;
- numerical cadence;
- Apollo 13 effectivity;
- Mission Control-facing routing.

## Next

Retrieve/open the digitized handbook scan and inspect its title/revision pages first. If it proves to be `LMA-790-2-LMS` Volume I, search the body for `DDP-224`, computer complex, common memory, program allocation, digital conversion, and guidance-computer simulation. Then pursue `67-16127` Section 7 for output ownership/interface mapping.
