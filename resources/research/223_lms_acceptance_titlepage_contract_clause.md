# Research note 223 — LMS acceptance title-page contract-clause lead

Date: 2026-09-16  
Status: **SECONDARY CATALOG TRANSCRIPTION OF PRIMARY TITLE PAGE; technical contents still not directly reviewed.**

## Question

Can the surviving `1L5-102-(H)` lead be narrowed beyond report number, title, date, contractor, and purchase-order lineage without inventing technical contents?

## Search result

A fresh search of the live Heritage Auctions lot for the surviving original manual exposes a more specific catalog transcription of the title page. The catalog states that the title page says the report was **“prepared and issued in compliance with P. O. 2-24481C, Section E, Paragraph 5.”**

The same catalog identifies the document as General Precision Incorporated, Link Group, Systems Division report `1L5-102-(H)`, *Apollo Lunar Module Simulator (LMS) Acceptance Test Procedures for Modification Kit No. 1*, dated 5 April 1967 and prepared for Grumman Aircraft Engineering Corporation.

This is consistent with research note 222's independent Singer/Link crosswalk tying `P.O. 2-24481C` to Grumman/NASA and the LEM application.

## What this closes

The retrieval lead can now include the contractual locator **Section E, Paragraph 5** in addition to `1L5-102-(H)` and `2-24481C`. That is useful for archival searches where the report number is absent but purchase-order correspondence or contract files survive.

## Evidence boundary

The title-page wording is presently available only through the auction catalog's transcription. The underlying title-page image/manual has not been directly inspected in this research pass, so the clause remains a **secondary transcription lead**, not promoted primary evidence.

Do **not** infer what Section E, Paragraph 5 required. In particular, this source does not establish:

- Modification Kit No. 1 hardware/software scope;
- acceptance observables, stimuli, tolerances, or pass/fail criteria;
- simulator output identifiers or units;
- relationship to LMA-790-2-LMS Volume II Section 7;
- 1968–1970 configuration continuity;
- Apollo 13 effectivity.
