# Research note 519 — Apollo 11 Flight Director-loop recovery path

Date: 2026-09-24

## Question

Can the next descent-alarm step be advanced without promoting a modern transcript or reconstructed chronology as primary evidence?

## Finding

Yes, but only at the source/provenance level. The Apollo Flight Journal's *Apollo 11: The Complete Descent* presentation is documented as combining original air/ground audio on one stereo channel with Mission Control Flight Director-loop audio on the other. This gives a practical listening route to the internal MOCR side of the alarm sequence and is independently useful for locating candidate utterances around the radio anchors established in note 518.

The presentation is nevertheless a later synchronization/compilation. Its subtitles, speaker labels, synchronization choices, and any modern transcription are not themselves 1969 primary records. They must not be used to establish exact wording, speaker identity, or clock offsets where those points are not independently verified from the underlying NASA recording.

The already cataloged NASA/JSC `792-AAI` Flight Director-loop recording remains the controlling primary artifact. The Apollo Flight Journal compilation is therefore a discovery/listening aid for that primary audio, not a replacement for it.

## Evidence-control consequence

Secondary published transcriptions of the loop contain candidate phrases such as Bales reporting a GO and discussion that the alarm was the same kind encountered previously. Those transcriptions are useful search cues only. They are deliberately **not promoted here as historical findings** because direct inspection of `792-AAI` / the underlying Flight Director-loop audio is still required.

Likewise, this source does not solve the Garman→Bales path: Garman's NASA oral history places his advice on a back-room voice loop, so absence or presence of his voice on the Flight Director loop cannot by itself name that circuit or establish GUIDO keyset privileges.

## Next discriminating action

1. Inspect the Flight Director-loop audio at the note-518 radio windows, beginning with the first 1202 and the later 1201.
2. Record only audible, independently attributable utterances; mark uncertain speakers as uncertain.
3. Reconcile the audio's timebase against the GOSS NET 1 anchors rather than assuming identical clocks.
4. Then inspect GUIDO L/R over the same windows to recover what is station-local versus propagated to Flight.

## Sources

- NASA/JSC `792-AAI`, Flight Director's Loop, Apollo 11 lunar descent, 1955–2025 (primary recording; cataloged previously in note 515).
- Apollo Flight Journal, *Apollo 11: The Complete Descent* (later synchronization/presentation of historical audio; discovery/listening aid).
- NASA, *Apollo 11 Technical Air-to-Ground Voice Transcription (GOSS NET 1)*, NTRS 20160014392 (primary radio timing anchors; note 518).
- NASA/JSC Oral History Project, John R. "Jack" Garman interview (primary retrospective testimony; note 514).

## Evidence status

- **DOCUMENTED:** a Flight Director-loop listening route exists in the Apollo Flight Journal compilation and is distinct from air/ground audio.
- **PRIMARY CONTROLLING ARTIFACT:** NASA/JSC `792-AAI`.
- **DISCOVERY ONLY:** compilation subtitles/speaker labels and third-party transcriptions.
- **OPEN:** exact internal wording/timing, uncertain speaker attribution, cross-loop clock reconciliation, Garman/Bales circuit identity.
