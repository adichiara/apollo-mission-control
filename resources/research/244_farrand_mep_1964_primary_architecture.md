# 244 — Farrand Mission Effects Projector 1964 primary architecture

Date: 2026-09-17
Status: primary technical architecture evidence recovered; LMS implementation identity remains open

## Question

Can the unresolved Mission Effects Projector architecture be constrained from a direct contemporaneous technical source while preserving the distinction between a Farrand MEP design and the specific 1967 Lunar Module Simulator implementation?

## Primary source recovered

NASA NTRS `19680013122` reproduces J. LaRussa, **The Infinity Image System in Visual Simulation**, presented at the AIAA/NASA Manned Space Flight Meeting in Houston, 4–6 November 1964 (AIAA Publication CP-10, pp. 263–270).

Direct NASA scan: https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19680013122/downloads/19680013122.pdf

The paper is direct contemporaneous technical evidence from Farrand's visual-simulation work and explicitly describes the Farrand approach and its Mission Effects Projector.

## Explicit findings

The paper states that the Farrand infinity-image approach could accept multiple image-generation inputs. It describes the **Mission Effects Projector (MEP)** as:

- a Farrand development;
- a **non-programmed continuous color film-strip projector**;
- capable of presenting an **earth-orbital view**, including moving cloud cover and sunrise/sunset simulations; and
- using **two film cassettes**, allowing orbital views at different scales.

This is the first recovered direct technical source in this thread that describes MEP projector architecture rather than only film-production support, bibliography, or later historical synthesis.

## Evidence boundary

The paper predates the July 1967 Grumman `LLR-400-329` LMS MEP program review and the August 1967 Farrand specification problem identified by NASA SP-4009. It describes a Farrand MEP design in the context of infinity-image visual simulation; it does **not**, by itself, prove that the specific LMS MEP installed at MSC or KSC in 1967 had this exact cassette arrangement, film content, controls, interfaces, or configuration.

Therefore do **not** infer from this source:

- that the 1967 LMS MEP was unchanged from the 1964 design;
- the exact LMS MEP optical/mechanical implementation;
- the specification(s) the later Farrand-designed/built LMS item failed;
- Itek contract award, hardware delivery, or replacement of Farrand hardware;
- any Farrand→Itek→Kollsman succession chain;
- MEP relationship to EVDE at the LMS system-interface level;
- DDP-224 ownership or control path;
- update cadence, scene-selection equations, or instructor controls;
- MSC/KSC configuration identity at a particular date; or
- Apollo 13 effectivity.

## Relationship to note 243

Aero Service NTRS `19690026999` independently establishes later LMS film-graphics production explicitly intended for MEP use and NASA/Grumman/Farrand coordination. The 1964 Farrand paper now supplies a direct technical description of an earlier MEP architecture. Together they support a real film-based MEP lineage, but they do not close the configuration gap between the 1964 design and the 1967–1969 LMS hardware.

## Consequence

The architecture question is narrowed rather than closed: a Farrand MEP design is directly documented as a continuous color film-strip projector with two scale-selecting cassettes, while the **specific deployed LMS MEP configuration remains unresolved**. Retrieval of `67-15490` / `LLR-400-329` is now even more important because it can establish what changed between the documented 1964 Farrand concept and the 1967 LMS program state. The 30 Aug. 1967 Bethpage weekly report remains the primary target for the specification-failure/proposed-Itek sequence.

No executable model constants, station product contracts, or maturity grades change from this finding.
