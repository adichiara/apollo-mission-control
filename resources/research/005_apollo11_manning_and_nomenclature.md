## Shift caution

The Apollo 11 manning document uses four MOCR shift columns.

The Apollo 13 baseline-data description says the MOCR/SSR functions were staffed by three 9-hour shifts.

Therefore shift organization changed or was represented differently by mission. Do not impose a single generic Apollo shift model.

## TELCOM / TELMU terminology

### Apollo 11 evidence

The official manning list labels the LM systems position:

**LM/TELCOM**

The Apollo 11 EVA Flight Director loop also contains direct calls such as:

- Flight -> Telcom
- Telcom -> Flight

### Oral-history evidence for later change

Robert H. Heselmeyer, who worked the LM systems discipline, recalled that the early LM flights used TELCOM and that the front-room call sign later changed to TELMU as EMU/lunar-surface responsibility became more significant. He associated TELMU with approximately Apollo 12 onward.

Source:

https://historycollection.jsc.nasa.gov/JSCHistoryPortal/history/oral_histories/HeselmeyerRH/HeselmeyerRH_11-12-04.pdf

### Current research conclusion

For an Apollo 11 simulation, **TELCOM** is the mission-specific label supported by the official manning list and Flight Director loop.

Do not substitute TELMU merely because it is a better-known later Apollo call sign.

## Apollo 11 landing-source terminology conflict

The modern Apollo Lunar Surface Journal commentary for the PDI GO/NO-GO sequence uses the label TELMU while other Apollo 11 sources use TELCOM.

Because the goal is mission-specific operational authenticity, the contemporary/official manning evidence and actual FD-loop call sign currently take precedence.

This is exactly the kind of source conflict that must remain visible in the research trail.

## Additional finding: training

The Apollo 11 press kit states that each crewman accumulated more than 400 hours in CSM/LM simulators and that this included closed-loop simulations with flight controllers in the MCC.

Source:

https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19690022248

This further supports treating integrated simulation behavior as a primary research topic rather than designing fictional malfunction exercises.
