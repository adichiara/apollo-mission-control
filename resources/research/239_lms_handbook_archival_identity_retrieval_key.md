# Research note 239 — LMS handbook archival identity and retrieval key

Date: 2026-09-17  
Status: **primary MSC bibliographic control recovered; handbook contents still not directly inspectable**

## Question

Can the next LMS machine-allocation retrieval target be identified precisely enough to move beyond broad title searches and request the actual configuration-era handbook?

## Primary-source-first result

Yes at the bibliographic level, but not yet at the content level.

The NASA Manned Spacecraft Center's *Apollo Engineering and Technology Index*, Volume I — Corporate Author Index (July 1968) lists the Grumman LMS instructor handbook under contract `NAS9-1100` and supplies MSC accession identifiers.

For `LMA-790-2-LMS`, the index identifies:

- **MSC accession `67-14186`** — *Lunar Module Mission Simulator Instructors Handbook, Volume I — Simulator Description*;
- **MSC accession `67-14187`** — Volume II, simulator-operation Sections 1 and 4;
- **MSC accession `67-14188`** — Volume II, simulator-operation Sections 5 and 6;
- **MSC accession `67-16127`** — Volume II, Section 7, *Simulator Output Tables*.

The July 1968 supplement repeats `67-14186` and `67-14187` and associates the handbook with Grumman and `NAS9-1100`.

Primary NASA bibliographic publication recovered as a scan/copy:

- *Apollo Engineering and Technology Index*, MSC-APOLLO-4, Vol. I, July 1968.
- Corporate-author entry: Grumman Aircraft Engineering Corporation.

## What this closes

The next archival target is no longer just a title string. Direct retrieval can be keyed by **MSC accession number**, report number, contract, and volume/section.

Priority order for the unresolved compute-allocation question:

1. `67-14186` — Volume I, Simulator Description;
2. `67-16127` — Volume II, Section 7, Simulator Output Tables;
3. `67-14187` / `67-14188` — operating sections if machine/program assignment is not present in Volume I.

This also confirms that the 1967 handbook belongs to Grumman's `NAS9-1100` documentation stream; it must not be conflated with the later Kollsman `NAS9-8634` LMS project-file trail.

## What remains unresolved

The bibliographic index does **not** expose the handbook body. Do not infer from its title or accession:

- the workloads of the two original LMS DDP-224 machines;
- software/program names or machine assignments;
- common-memory map or execution cadence;
- whether Section 7 output tables identify computer ownership;
- Apollo 13 configuration effectivity;
- a relationship between a handbook-defined three-machine architecture and Jackson's later four-DDP-224 room recollection.

A direct copy of the handbook or another configuration-controlled record is still required for those claims.

## Retrieval consequence

Future searches/archival requests should use the accession identifiers first, especially `67-14186` and `67-16127`, together with `LMA-790-2-LMS` and `NAS9-1100`. This is materially stronger than generic web searches for the handbook title.

## Architecture/product consequence

None. This note improves provenance and retrieval precision only. No executable model constant, station product, machine workload, or Apollo 13-specific configuration changes from this pass.
