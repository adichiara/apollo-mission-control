# 801 — Apollo 11 lunar-descent Flight Director loop archive

Date: 2026-09-24
Research thread: `apollo11-descent-audio-provenance`

## Question

Is there an agency-cataloged primary audio source, independent of the restored GUIDO L/R corpus, that can verify what reached the Flight Director loop during Apollo 11 lunar descent?

## Primary-source result

Yes. The Defense Visual Information Distribution Service (DVIDS) hosts the NASA Apollo 11 audio collection and identifies NASA as the courtesy source. Its catalog metadata states that item `792-AAI` contains the **Flight Director's Loop** for three Apollo 11 intervals, including **Lunar Descent 1955–2025**. The page further states that the material was digitized, cataloged, and archived by the Houston Audio Control Room at NASA Johnson Space Center under NASA identifier `Apollo11Audio`.

This is a distinct primary-audio recovery route for the lunar-descent front-room/Flight-loop record. It is useful for checking which calls reached the Flight Director loop and for comparing the timing/order of front-room calls against GUIDO L/R station recordings.

## Boundary

The DVIDS catalog entry identifies the recording and its lunar-descent coverage, but catalog metadata alone does **not** establish the exact words, speakers, timing, overlap, or the back-room loop used by Garman. Those claims require listening to the audio itself.

A voice heard on the Flight Director loop also does not establish the originating back-room circuit or a station's complete keyset privilege matrix. In particular, this source does not convert Garman's unnamed `back-room voice loop` into `FD LOOP`.

The DVIDS item page exposes audio/transcript download controls, but this research pass does not treat any machine or derivative transcript as authority. The primary recording remains the evidence target.

## Simulator consequence

The alarm-decision sequence can now be verified from two complementary primary-recording perspectives:

1. GUIDO L/R station recordings for the station-local audible sequence; and
2. NASA `792-AAI` Flight Director-loop lunar-descent audio for calls that reached the Flight loop.

Cross-comparison can establish ordering and relay behavior more strongly than either recording alone while preserving the unresolved named back-room-loop question.
