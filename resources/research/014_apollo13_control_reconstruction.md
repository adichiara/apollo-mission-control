# Research Note 014 — Apollo 13 LM CONTROL Reconstruction

**Date:** 2026-09-11  
**Status:** REVIEWED-PARTIAL

## Key result

Apollo 13's CONTROL appendix provides unusually strong **workflow and decision-rule evidence**, even though exact CRT layouts remain missing.

## Pre-accident SHe case

The LM entered flight with a known concern over DPS supercritical-helium pressure rise.

CONTROL had a documented branching plan tied to actual pressure:

- 660–770 psia: acceptable
- 770–800 psia: recheck later
- >800 psia: periodic telemetry and ground trend/extrapolation
- predicted ≥1800 psia at PDI: proposed short DPS firing and SHe vent

Actual LM-entry indication was about 710–720 psia.

This is an excellent example of historically authentic simulator difficulty generated by thresholds/trends rather than a scripted game event.

## Post-accident CONTROL work

After the CSM anomaly, CONTROL supported:

- LM control-system activation
- DPS/RCS preparation
- MCC-3 DPS burn
- RCS usage monitoring
- DAP deadband modification
- +X jet disabling for propellant conservation
- PC+2 burn rule monitoring
- GDA/rate/attitude monitoring
- PTC establishment
- later midcourse maneuver configuration choices

## Mission-rule evidence

For PC+2, CONTROL records explicit shutdown criteria, including one fuel/oxidizer inlet differential criterion described as **ground callout only**.

This supports maintaining different information availability between crew and ground.

## Information-quality evidence

An RCS thrust-chamber-pressure switch failed closed and compromised a caution/warning failed-off indication.

This reinforces the broader simulation rule that indicator state and physical hardware state must remain separable.

## Current limitation

No exact Apollo 13 CONTROL display-format inventory has yet been located.

Do not create a generic LM control page from the parameter list alone.

## Source

Apollo 13 Mission Operations Report, Appendix H.
