# 243 — LMS Mission Effects Projector film-graphics primary source

Date: 2026-09-17
Status: primary technical source recovered; visual-hardware/vendor succession remains open

## Question

Can the newly identified MEP thread be advanced from bibliographic metadata to direct technical evidence without inferring the unrecovered contents of `LLR-400-329` / `67-15490`?

## Primary source recovered

NASA NTRS `19690026999` is Aero Service Corporation's **Final Report — LEM Mission Simulator Film Graphics**, Contract `NAS 9-5981`, July 1969, prepared for NASA Manned Spacecraft Center.

Direct NASA scan: https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19690026999/downloads/19690026999.pdf

The report is contractor-produced technical documentation delivered to NASA and therefore direct primary evidence for the film-graphics work.

## Explicit findings

The report establishes that:

- Aero Service produced terrestrial and lunar film graphics for the LEM/Lunar Module Mission Simulator program under `NAS 9-5981`.
- Final terrestrial-film contrast was selected at a meeting at **Farrand Optical Company** with NASA and Grumman representatives.
- The report describes NASA-directed test-film work and records that on **30 November 1967** Contract Change Authorization (CCA) No. 3 required Aero Service to produce enhanced lunar Range 4 film samples using Lunar Orbiter photography.
- CCA No. 3 explicitly required that the enhancement technique be capable of application to production of film graphics **for use in the Mission Effects Projector**.
- Aero Service delivered the black-and-white and magenta enhanced-detail samples on **11 January 1968** and submitted a report on that work in January 1968.

These statements directly connect LMS film-graphics production, Farrand/NASA/Grumman coordination, and the Mission Effects Projector. They also establish that the MEP's lunar visual content was being deliberately updated to exploit Lunar Orbiter imagery by late 1967/early 1968.

## Corroborating NASA historical synthesis

NASA SP-4009, *The Apollo Spacecraft: A Chronology*, Volume IV, records for the week ending 25 August 1967 that Grumman sought outside optomechanical help because a Farrand-designed-and-built MEP did not meet established specifications; it says Itek's technical proposal was judged most acceptable and NASA concurred in a proposed letter contract. SP-4009 cites the underlying `MSC, BMR Bethpage, Weekly Activities Report, Week Ending August 25, 1967`, dated 30 August 1967.

This is authoritative NASA historical synthesis with an identified primary record, but the underlying weekly report has not yet been recovered in this pass. Accordingly, retain the Farrand-performance/Itek-selection sequence as corroborated retrieval guidance rather than promoting it to a configuration-controlled vendor-succession fact.

## What this closes

The MEP was not merely an abstract projector label in later archival descriptions. Direct technical documentation shows a film-graphics production pipeline supporting it, with terrestrial/lunar imagery, NASA/Grumman/Farrand coordination, and Lunar Orbiter-driven lunar-detail enhancement.

## What remains open

- EVDE ownership or architecture;

- MSC/KSC configuration differences;
- Apollo 13 effectivity;
- any relationship between `NAS 9-5981`, `NAS9-1100`, and later Kollsman `NAS9-8634` beyond what an opened contract/configuration source explicitly states.

## Consequence

Add NTRS `19690026999` as a direct MEP technical source. Continue retrieval of `LLR-400-329` / `67-15490`, and separately target the cited 30 August 1967 Bethpage weekly activity report to move the Farrand/Itek sequence from NASA synthesis back to its underlying primary record.
