# Research note 227 — LMS Mission Effects Projector primary context

Date: 2026-09-17
Status: **primary-source context recovered; `LLR-400-329` still not recovered**

## Question

Can primary NASA sources establish anything about the Mission Effects Projector beyond the title of Grumman `LLR-400-329` without inventing a Section 7 data path or Apollo 13 configuration?

## Primary-source results

### Apollo chronology

NASA SP-4009, *The Apollo Spacecraft: A Chronology*, records for the week ending 25 August 1967 that Grumman proposed procurement of a study of the **mission effects projector**. The chronology says the item had been **designed and built by Farrand** but **did not meet the established specifications**. Grumman sought optomechanical assistance; proposals were received from Itek, Biorad, Link, and Conductron; Grumman considered Itek's proposal most technically acceptable and proposed a letter contract, in which NASA concurred.

The chronology cites the underlying contemporaneous record as MSC, BMR Bethpage, *Weekly Activities Report, Week Ending August 25, 1967*, 30 Aug 1967.

Primary NASA copy: https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/sp-4009vol4.pdf

### NASA visual-simulation technical paper

NASA/NTRS record 19680013122, J. LaRussa, *The Infinity Image System in Visual Simulation*, describes Farrand's Mission Effects Projector as one of the image-generation inputs designed into the Farrand virtual-image display system. The paper describes a continuous-color-film approach for orbital imagery and sunrise/sunset simulation. This is useful technology-family context, but it predates the 1967 LMS program-review record and does not establish the delivered LMS configuration.

Primary NTRS copy: https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19680013122/downloads/19680013122.pdf

### KSC simulator description

NASA/KSC report KSC-130-68 describes mission-simulator peripheral equipment and states that a mission effects projector supplied Earth and/or lunar scenes depending on mission or spacecraft attitude. It separately describes digital-conversion equipment handling interfaces among instructor station, crew station, computers, and visual equipment.

Primary NASA copy: https://www3.nasa.gov/centers/kennedy/pdf/744319main_1968.pdf

## What this closes

The Mission Effects Projector was not merely an unexplained title phrase in the MSC index. Primary NASA material establishes a Farrand visual-simulation technology family and, by August 1967, a Grumman MEP item whose performance against established specifications was sufficiently problematic to trigger a specialist study procurement.

This materially strengthens the reason to retrieve `LLR-400-329` (7 Jun 1967): it predates the documented August specification problem and may contain relevant program-review evidence. That possibility is a retrieval rationale only, not a claim about the report's contents.

## Evidence boundary

Do **not** infer from these sources:

- which requirements the LMS MEP failed;
- numerical tolerances or acceptance criteria;
- that `LLR-400-329` documents the later August problem;
- a specific Section 7 output-to-MEP signal mapping;
- controller-visible station routing;
- that the 1967 configuration survived unchanged into Apollo 13 training in 1970.
