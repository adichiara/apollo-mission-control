# Research note 229 — RG 255 LMS visual-contract retrieval keys

Date: 2026-09-17
Status: **primary archival locator strengthened; configuration relationship remains unresolved**

## Question

Does the primary archival inventory provide a later Apollo-era retrieval path for the LMS Mission Effects Projector / external visual equipment beyond the 1967 Farrand program-review trail?

## Primary-source result

The NARA/NASA RG 255 preliminary inventory provides two adjacent but distinct simulator holdings:

- **E.155B — Reference Files on the Lunar Landing Training Vehicle, 1962–1973**: the series description explicitly says it contains some material relating to the **Mission Effects Projector (MEP) and External Visual Display Equipment (EVDE) of the Lunar Module mission simulator**. The inventory gives old accession numbers `72A797` and `73ABC1675` and shelf/location references `B-28-34-2`, `B-35-28-12`, `B-35-29-2, 4-7`, and `V-10-6-6`.
- **E.155B1 — Project Files on the Lunar Module Simulator, 1968–1970**: the series contains correspondence, memoranda, statement of work, program/procurement plans, acceptance test plans and procedures, progress/technical reports, operations manuals, photographs of surface models, and copies of drawings. The inventory states that **most correspondence is with Kollsman Instrument Corporation** and relates to administration of **contract `NAS9-8634` for construction of LM simulators at both MSC and KSC**. It gives old accession `72A794` and location `A-23-16-6`.

Primary inventory: https://www.ibiblio.org/apollo/NARA-SW/Rg255-1.pdf (RG 255, p. 25 in the inventory pagination).

## What this changes

The MEP/EVDE retrieval path is no longer limited to Grumman `LLR-400-329`, Farrand, or the Link acceptance-document family. The primary inventory itself names MEP/EVDE material in E.155B and separately identifies a 1968–1970 LMS project-file series with a specific later contract (`NAS9-8634`) and contractor (Kollsman).

This creates a concrete archival crosswalk to pursue:

`1967 Farrand/Grumman MEP → E.155B MEP/EVDE reference material → 1968–1970 E.155B1 LMS project/acceptance files → Kollsman NAS9-8634`

That arrow sequence is a **retrieval plan**, not a proven hardware succession.

## Evidence boundary

The inventory does **not** establish that:

- Kollsman replaced Farrand as MEP supplier;
- `NAS9-8634` specifically covered the MEP or EVDE rather than other LMS construction work;
- the E.155B MEP/EVDE files describe the same unit as `LLR-400-329`;
- the 1967 specification problem caused the later Kollsman contract;
- either holding exposes LMS Section 7 outputs or Mission Control station data paths;
- the described hardware/configuration was the Apollo 13 training configuration.

Do not promote any of those relationships without direct file-level evidence.

2. Request/search **E.155B1** using `NAS9-8634`, Kollsman, MEP, EVDE, acceptance, statement of work, and surface-model terms.
3. On recovery, build a dated configuration/vendor matrix and connect records only where document text supplies an explicit relationship.
4. Continue direct retrieval of `LLR-400-329` / `*67-15490` and Section 7 / `*67-16127` in parallel.
