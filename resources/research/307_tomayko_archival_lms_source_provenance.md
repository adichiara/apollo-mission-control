# Research 307 — Tomayko archival LMS source provenance

Date: 2026-09-18

## Question

Can the source trail behind Tomayko's Apollo mission-simulator computer discussion be narrowed to recoverable archival records without promoting the 1988 synthesis itself to primary evidence?

## Result

Yes. Wichita State University Special Collections' finding aid for the **Dr. James E. Tomayko Collection of NASA Documents**, call number `MS 87-08`, identifies a compact Apollo simulator source cluster in **Box 40, folders 87–98**. The collection description states that Tomayko's NASA report *Computers in Spaceflight* was based on archival materials held in the collection.

Most important to the current unresolved DDP-224 procurement/topology work:

- **Box 40, FF 87** — *Lunar Module Mission Simulator Pamphlet*.
- **Box 40, FF 88** — letter from Donald K. Slayton to BG/Procurement and Contracts Division.
- **Box 40, FF 90** — *News NASA. Contract Sets. Six Computers for Apollo Use.*
- **Box 40, FF 96** — *Subcontractors On Apollo and Lunar Mission Simulators*.
- **Box 40, FF 97** — *Mission Simulator Design in Support of Space Programs*.
- **Box 40, FF 98** — *Information on Apollo Mission Simulator and Lunar Mission Simulator*.

FF 90 is an especially strong retrieval lead for the unresolved Honeywell award/date problem because its archival title independently matches the six-computer/complex procurement topic discussed by Tomayko. It may be the contemporaneous news item underlying his treatment, but **that identity is not yet proven** because the folder contents have not been inspected.

## Evidence class

The Wichita finding aid is archival metadata/provenance, not the technical primary source itself. It establishes that these records exist in Tomayko's research collection and provides exact box/folder retrieval coordinates. It does not establish their internal wording, dates, authorship beyond the supplied folder descriptions, or technical claims.

NASA SP-4009 remains the stronger currently inspected source for the contemporaneous `66-254` citation: it explicitly cites NASA News Release `66-254`, 21 September 1966, and describes six separate computer complexes. Tomayko's 1988 NASA report gives 21 July 1966. The Box 40 FF 90 record now provides a concrete archival path for testing that discrepancy rather than resolving it by inference.

## Important boundary

Do **not** infer from the phrase “Six Computers” in the FF 90 folder title that the Honeywell procurement consisted of exactly six DDP-224 processors. SP-4009's inspected wording is “six separate computer complexes,” and the original FF 90 item has not been read. Likewise, the nearby LMS pamphlet and simulator-information folders cannot yet be used to assign machine workloads or reconcile the two/three/four-machine evidence.
