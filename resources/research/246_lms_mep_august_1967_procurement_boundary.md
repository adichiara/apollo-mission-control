# 246 — LMS MEP August 1967 procurement boundary

Date: 2026-09-17
Status: authoritative NASA synthesis recovered; cited primary weekly report remains unrecovered

## Question

What can be established about the August 1967 response to the Farrand Mission Effects Projector specification problem without inventing a Farrand→Itek→Kollsman hardware succession?

## Source recovered

NASA SP-4009, *The Apollo Spacecraft: A Chronology*, Volume IV, entry for week ending 25 August 1967.

NASA-hosted volume: https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/sp-4009vol4.pdf

Web transcription: https://www.apolloproject.com/sp-4009/asc-v4-15.htm

The chronology cites the underlying contemporaneous record as:

`MSC, BMR Bethpage, "Weekly Activities Report, Week Ending August 25, 1967," Aug. 30, 1967.`

The weekly report itself has not yet been recovered, so the findings below remain authoritative NASA historical synthesis rather than primary-source closure.

## Explicit findings

NASA records that Grumman proposed procurement of a **study** concerning a Mission Effects Projector that had been designed and built by Farrand but did not meet established specifications.

For that study:

- Grumman approached 15 optomechanical firms;
- seven expressed interest: Itek, Kollmorgen, Bausch & Lomb, Kollsman Instrument, Biorad, General Precision Link Group, and Conductron;
- technical proposals were received from Itek, Biorad, Link, and Conductron;
- Grumman judged Itek's proposal the most technically acceptable; and
- Grumman proposed a letter contract for Itek, in which NASA concurred.

## Boundary clarified

This materially narrows the vendor-history question.

Kollsman was an **interested firm approached in the August 1967 study procurement**, but the NASA chronology does not say Kollsman submitted a technical proposal or received this study award. Itek was the firm Grumman selected for the proposed letter contract.

This therefore argues against treating later Kollsman `NAS9-8634` records as evidence that Kollsman directly succeeded Farrand in this August 1967 action. A later Kollsman role may still have existed, but it requires its own contracting/configuration evidence.

Likewise, the entry concerns procurement of a **study**. It does not establish that Itek manufactured, rebuilt, replaced, accepted, or operated the deployed MEP.

## Not established

Do not infer from this source:

- the failed Farrand specification or numerical acceptance tolerance;
- exact MEP/EVDE hardware configuration;
- that Itek replaced Farrand hardware;
- that Kollsman replaced Itek;
- a Farrand→Itek→Kollsman succession;
- computer ownership or DDP-224 interfaces;
- MSC/KSC site effectivity;
- Apollo 13 configuration; or
- Mission Control routing/product effects.

## Consequence

The vendor-history hypothesis is now more tightly bounded: **Farrand-built MEP specification problem → Grumman competitive study solicitation → Itek selected for proposed study letter contract**, at NASA-synthesis evidence level. Kollsman's presence in the solicitation is not evidence of award.

The cited 30 Aug. 1967 Bethpage weekly report remains the next primary target. Recovery should verify the chronology wording and may expose the study scope, specification problem, procurement identifier, or follow-on path. `67-15490` / `LLR-400-329` remains the immediately preceding July 1967 technical-program target.

No executable model constant, station product contract, or maturity grade changes from this finding.
