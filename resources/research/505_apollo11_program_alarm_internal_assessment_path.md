# 505 — Apollo 11 program-alarm internal assessment path

Date: 2026-09-23

## Question

Can the unresolved internal assessment ownership for the Apollo 11 1201/1202 alarms be narrowed without inferring routing from display availability?

## Primary-participant evidence

Yes. NASA's Johnson Space Center Oral History Project interview with John R. "Jack" Garman (27 March 2001) records Garman's firsthand account of the alarm episode and explicitly distinguishes the front room from the back room. Garman describes himself and colleagues in the back room as helping Steve Bales, and notes that the front room could not hear the back-room conversation because it was operating on the Flight Director's loop.

NASA's oral-history record identifies Garman's Apollo-era role in the Guidance Software Section / Flight Software Division. This is direct participant evidence for a support-room-to-GUIDO assessment path, not merely a later narrative assigning credit.

NASA's agency history summary of Garman independently states the operational chain in explicit terms: Steve Bales was the Guidance Officer in Mission Control; Bales called Jack Garman, who was working in the back room supporting him; Garman recognized the 1202 and advised that landing could continue. This is consistent with Garman's firsthand oral-history account.

## Consequence

The previously broad internal ownership question can be narrowed:

`guidance-software back-room support (Garman/team) → GUIDO (Steve Bales) → FLIGHT/ground decision chain → CAPCOM → crew`

The first two links are now supported by NASA participant/agency evidence; the CAPCOM-to-crew link was already established directly by the Apollo 11 air-to-ground transcript in note 504.

This does **not** establish the exact voice-loop name/channel, console keying, DRK/display request, timing of every individual alarm call, or whether Garman alone originated every later 1201/1202 recommendation. Those details remain open.

## Evidence status

- **DOCUMENTED / SUFFICIENT:** guidance-software back-room support assisted GUIDO Steve Bales in assessing the Apollo 11 program alarms.
- **DOCUMENTED / SUFFICIENT:** GUIDO is the front-room position in this assessment chain; CAPCOM communicates the resulting ground disposition to the crew.
- **UNRESOLVED:** exact internal loop/channel identity, keying/routing mechanics, and per-alarm detailed call sequence.
- **NO INFERENCE:** MSK-1137 field availability is not used as evidence of station ownership.
