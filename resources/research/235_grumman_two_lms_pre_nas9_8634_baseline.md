# Research note 235 — Grumman two-LMS baseline predates NAS9-8634 project files

Date: 2026-09-17
Status: **primary program chronology establishes earlier two-simulator obligation; NAS9-8634 implementation meaning still unresolved**

## Question

Does the E.155B1 description of `NAS9-8634` as being for “construction of LM simulators at both MSC and KSC” establish that this later Kollsman contract originated the two LMS installations?

## Primary sources

1. NASA, *The Apollo Spacecraft — A Chronology*, Vol. IV, February 15, 1966 entry. NASA records that conversion of the Grumman LEM development contract to a four-year cost-plus-incentive agreement required Grumman to deliver **15 flight articles, 10 test articles, and 2 mission simulators**. The chronology cites NASA Headquarters TWX, “NASA Converts Apollo Contract to Cost-Plus-Incentive,” February 15, 1966.
   - NASA/NTRS scan: https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19800011953/downloads/19800011953.pdf
2. NASA, *Astronautics and Aeronautics, 1966*, February 15 entry, independently records the same Grumman contract conversion and the requirement for **two mission simulators**, citing NASA Release 66-37.
   - https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/1966.pdf
3. NASA/NARA RG 255 preliminary inventory, E.155B1, describes 1968–1970 project files whose Kollsman correspondence concerns administration of `NAS9-8634` for construction of LM simulators at MSC and KSC.
   - https://www.ibiblio.org/apollo/NARA-SW/Rg255-1.pdf

## Finding

NASA's primary 1966 program record establishes that **two LEM mission simulators were already contractual Grumman deliverables by February 15, 1966**, well before the 1968–1970 date span of the surviving E.155B1 Kollsman / `NAS9-8634` project files.

This is an important chronology constraint on the word **“construction”** in the E.155B1 inventory description. `NAS9-8634` must not be described, solely from that inventory wording, as the original procurement or origin of the Apollo LM mission-simulator requirement. An earlier Grumman contractual obligation for two mission simulators is directly documented.

## What this does not establish

The evidence does **not** establish what `NAS9-8634` actually delivered. It does not show whether Kollsman's work was new construction, reconstruction, duplication, replacement, relocation, modernization, subsystem installation, site-specific completion, or some other implementation. It also does not establish whether the two simulators named in the 1966 Grumman obligation map one-for-one to the later MSC and KSC installations without intervening configuration or procurement changes.

No MEP, EVDE, AOT, Farrand/Itek, Section 7, Mission Control routing, or Apollo 13 effectivity claim is promoted from this chronology.

## Retrieval consequence

Direct E.155B1 recovery should now ask a sharper question: **what did `NAS9-8634` construct or modify relative to the already-existing Grumman two-mission-simulator obligation?** Prioritize the statement of work, procurement/program plans, contract correspondence, acceptance plans/procedures, and dated drawings. Extract explicit predecessor/configuration language rather than inferring it from dates or vendor names.
