# Research Note 026 — Mission G Flight-Dynamics Display Family

**Date:** 2026-09-11  
**Status:** CROSS-MISSION / APOLLO 11 EVIDENCE

## Source

**Project Apollo 500 RTCC Operations Support Plan for Mission G**  
MSC Internal Note 69-FS-2  
April 1969

https://www.ibiblio.org/apollo/Documents/RTCC%20Operations%20Support%20Plan%20for%20Mission%20G.pdf

## Objective

Identify actual RTCC display numbers used in flight-dynamics support, providing a concrete display family for comparison with Apollo 13.

## Documented displays

### Predicted Site Acquisition Table

**MSK 55 and 56**

The RTCC operator was instructed to monitor these tables and update them with MED **U15** on request from FDO or Data Select.

Crucial behavior:

> The display did not automatically update after trajectory updates.

After a trajectory update, the display could therefore contain incorrect/outdated acquisition information until U15 was reentered.

### Experimental Site Acquisition

**MSK 1506**

Generated using MED **U16** on direction from Flight Crew / RTCC Dynamics.

### Sunrise / Sunset

**MSK 1502**

Updated on display request.

MED **U08** changed starting time or revolution.

### Moonrise / Moonset

**MSK 1501**

Updated through manual entry **U07**.

## Additional FDO / RTCC workflow

The Mission G plan describes RTCC Dynamics personnel supporting FDO by:

- using MED U12 for predicted apogee/perigee;
- U13 for predicted geographic-longitude crossing time;
- U14 for longitude at a specified time;
- displaying desired vectors/related parameters through U02;
- notifying FDO when the trajectory became impacting;
- handling vector/reference-frame requests.

## Simulation significance

This gives us another documented example of a real Mission Control display being:

- generated by explicit operator input;
- dependent on current RTCC trajectory state;
- capable of becoming stale after that underlying state changes.

A future FIDO/NETWORK display should therefore not necessarily auto-refresh every derived product just because the server knows the new trajectory.

## Apollo 13 status

These identifiers are Mission G / Apollo 11 evidence.

They are useful as a documented RTCC display family and continuity target, but they are **not yet confirmed as Apollo 13 display numbers**.

## Research target

Locate Apollo 13/H-2 RTCC operations/configuration documentation and determine:

- whether MSKs 55/56, 1501, 1502, 1506 persisted;
- whether their content/refresh behavior changed;
- which front-room or RTCC positions normally requested/observed them.
