# Research note 305 — LMS-2 KSC site identity

Date: 2026-09-18  
Status: **PRIMARY CREW-TRAINING RECORD CLOSES LMS-2 = KSC SITE IDENTITY; MACHINE/FUNCTION ALLOCATION STILL UNRESOLVED**

## Question

Can a contemporaneous primary NASA training record independently establish which Lunar Module Mission Simulator number was the Kennedy Space Center installation?

## Primary source

NASA Flight Crew Operations training material, *Apollo 11 Activity Planning Guide*, prepared by Training Coordinator Lloyd Reeder, 9 May 1969, document `HSI-208973`.

NASA-hosted scan:
https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/static/history/alsj/a11/A11CrewTraining.pdf

The dated activity grid repeatedly identifies LMS use. Most importantly, the 19 May entry explicitly pairs **“LOI Sim-KSC”** with **“LMS #2”**. The following Apollo 10 flight-period entries continue to schedule `LMS #2`, while other dates distinguish `LMS #1` and `LMS #2` as separate simulator resources.

## What this establishes

This is contemporaneous NASA operational/training evidence that **LMS #2 was the KSC Lunar Module Mission Simulator** by May 1969.

That independently strengthens the site interpretation of KSC-GP-421F (research note 304): when the 1973 KSC report refers to DDP-224 systems formerly used in the Apollo `LMS-2 system`, the simulator identifier is consistent with the KSC training installation rather than being merely an unlocated LMS designation.

## What it does not establish

Do not infer from the planning guide that:

- LMS #1's site is proved by this specific entry;
- LMS #2's May 1969 computer count or program allocation is known;
- the two DDP-224 systems retained in 1973 were the complete LMS-2 Apollo-era computer set;
- a particular DDP-224 ran vehicle dynamics, systems, LGC/PGNCS simulation, visual control, or another workload;
- the May 1969 configuration is unchanged for Apollo 13;
- the guide establishes serial numbers, memory configuration, update cadence, maintenance state, or simulator hardware effectivity.

The source closes a **site/identifier crosswalk**, not the unresolved compute-topology questions.

## Relationship to existing evidence

- NASA TN D-7112 remains the primary technical authority for the mature three-machine LMS functional architecture and the guidance-dedicated machine.
- KSC-GP-421F remains the primary post-Apollo disposition evidence for two surviving LMS-2-provenance DDP-224 systems.
- The Apollo 11 Activity Planning Guide now supplies an independent contemporaneous operational anchor that places `LMS #2` at KSC.
- Jackson's retrospective four-machine Houston-room observation remains a separate physical-room question and is not affected by this site crosswalk.

## Project consequence

The source catalog may now state **LMS-2 = KSC** for the May 1969 operational context with primary support. This removes site identity from the list of ambiguities surrounding the 1973 disposition record.

No executable constants, station maturity grades, processor/workload assignments, or Apollo 13 effectivity claims change.
