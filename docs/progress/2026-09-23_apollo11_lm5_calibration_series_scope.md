# Progress — LM-5 operational-calibration series scope

Date: 2026-09-23

## Completed

Researched the next unresolved CONTROL target from the current stopping point: the mission-specific **Operational Calibration Curves, Vol. II — Calibration Curves for LM-5 (12 Jun 1969)**.

A primary NASA/NTRS catalog record for the later Apollo 16 operational-calibration series (NASA-TM-X-68322; NTRS 19720018200) explicitly describes the family as calibration of **PCM downlink and onboard-display measurements**, including measurement identification, multi-mode calibration summaries, bilevel-event listings, and instrumentation/meter-link curves.

This changes the search interpretation without changing any historical value: the LM-5 volume remains an important vehicle-specific CONTROL/telemetry source, but it must not be presumed to be an engine-performance or FTP-force calibration document merely from its title. The existing NARA Fort Worth recovery route remains valid; its priority is now channel/measurement verification rather than assumed engine-force recovery.

The direct FTP source therefore moves ahead of it: **John P. Mayer, MSC/MPAD memorandum 69-FMZ2-149, 5 Jun 1969**, explicitly titled “Effect of Fixed Throttle Point Thrust on the Time from Loss of Radial Guidance Control to DPS Throttle-down.”

## 23 Sep continuation — direct recovery coordinates

Primary archival research at Purdue narrowed that Mayer target to **Neil A. Armstrong papers, MSA 5, Series 1, Box 58, Folder 4, Item 10**. Purdue also supplies the hierarchical identifier **MSA 5, Series 1, Sub-Series 5, Sub-Group 4, File 6, Item 16**. The item title and 5 Jun 1969 date match the independent NASA/MSC bibliographic citation to 69-FMZ2-149.

The two 8 Jul 1969 companion throttle-down studies are physically located in **MSA 5, Box 54, Folder 5, Items 14–15**. Purdue explicitly identifies Item 15 (“Effects of known dispersions at PDI on throttle-down time”) as **MSA 5, Series 1, Sub-Series 5, Sub-Group 4, File 2, Item 21**. I did not extrapolate a hierarchical item number for Item 14.

This is a useful stopping-point advance: the next discriminating action is no longer generic web/NTRS searching, but recovery/reproduction of those three specific Armstrong archival items. Public searching in this run did not recover their underlying pages.

The Purdue Armstrong catalog also exposes an **Apollo 11/LM-5 Post Mission Report, 5 Aug 1969** archival item. Its underlying pages remain unrecovered, so it is retained only as a secondary LM-5-specific archival lead.

## Boundary preserved

No FTP force, dispersion, LM weight, PDI value, or throttle-down timing is inferred from archival titles. No later-mission numerical calibration is transferred to LM-5. No LM-5 telemetry channel is invented. Exact FTP force, delivered thrust/Isp, and PDI mass remain unresolved.
