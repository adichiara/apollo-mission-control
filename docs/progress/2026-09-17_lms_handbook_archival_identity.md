# Progress — LMS handbook archival identity

Date: 2026-09-17

## Completed

Research notes 239–246 continued the unresolved LMS machine-allocation and visual-system thread with primary-source-first retrieval.

The NASA Manned Spacecraft Center's July 1968 *Apollo Engineering and Technology Index* supplies exact MSC accession identifiers for the Grumman `LMA-790-2-LMS` handbook family under `NAS9-1100`: `67-14186` (Volume I, Simulator Description), `67-14187` and `67-14188` (Volume II operating sections), and `67-16127` (Volume II, Section 7, Simulator Output Tables).

Research note 240 located a public digitized candidate with the matching Volume I title in the Virtual AGC collection. The surfaced discovery metadata does not expose enough scan-level identity to treat it as recovered `67-14186`.

Research note 241 added independent NASA/KSC GP-642 confirmation mapping the exact Volume I title to GAEC `LMA 790-2-LMS`, dated Apr. 1, 1967. The date discrepancy remains preserved: the MSC main index and GP-642 support 01-04-67, while the MSC supplement gives 15-05-67.

Research note 242 adds a second exact primary retrieval key from the NASA/MSC corporate-author index: Grumman `LLR-400-329`, **Contract NAS9-1100, LMS Mission Effects Projector Program Review**, dated 06-07-67, MSC accession `67-15490`. This establishes the existence and archival identity of a contemporaneous Grumman MEP program review, but not its technical contents or relationship to later Kollsman `NAS9-8634` work.

Research note 243 recovered direct technical evidence in NASA NTRS `19690026999`, Aero Service Corporation's July 1969 *Final Report — LEM Mission Simulator Film Graphics*, Contract `NAS 9-5981`. It explicitly connects terrestrial/lunar LMS film graphics to the Mission Effects Projector, records NASA/Grumman/Farrand coordination, and reproduces a 30 Nov. 1967 CCA requiring a Lunar-Orbiter-based enhancement technique applicable to MEP film production.

Research note 244 recovered direct contemporaneous MEP architecture evidence in J. LaRussa's 1964 AIAA/NASA paper, NASA NTRS `19680013122`. It constrains the Farrand design lineage but is not treated as proof of the exact deployed MSC/KSC LMS configuration.

Research note 245 returned to primary NASA TN D-7112 and recovered the mature LMS near-body visual chain directly: common filmstrip → zoom optics → four screens → articulated probes, with altitude-dependent distortion/horizon mechanisms and documented fidelity limits.

Research note 246 tightened the August 1967 vendor/procurement boundary using NASA SP-4009 while explicitly retaining its evidence class as an authoritative synthesis pending recovery of its cited Bethpage weekly report. NASA records a Farrand-designed/built MEP that failed established specifications, followed by a Grumman solicitation for an optomechanical **study**. Kollsman was among seven interested firms, but the listed technical proposals came from Itek, Biorad, Link, and Conductron; Grumman selected Itek's proposal for a proposed letter contract and NASA concurred. This is evidence against silently treating Kollsman's later `NAS9-8634` records as the direct August 1967 successor action, and it does not establish that Itek manufactured or replaced deployed hardware.

No workload, cadence, exact 1967 projector configuration, EVDE relationship, Mission Control routing, acceptance tolerance, hardware vendor succession, or Apollo 13 configuration claim was promoted.
