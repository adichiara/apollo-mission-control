# Roadmap continuation — LM-5 operational-calibration series scope

Date: 2026-09-23
Parent: `docs/roadmap/2026-09-22_apollo11_descent_dynamics_input_audit.md`

## Question

Does the identified 12 Jun 1969 **Operational Calibration Curves, Vol. II — Calibration Curves for LM-5** provide enough evidence to treat it as the likely source of the unresolved DPS fixed-throttle-point force calibration?

## New primary-series evidence

A public NTRS record for the later **Apollo 16/AS-511/LM-11 Operational Calibration Curves** (NASA-TM-X-68322, NTRS 19720018200) describes this document family as calibration curves for **pulse-code-modulation downlink and onboard display**. Its abstract identifies measurement-calibration format, measurement identification, multi-mode calibration summaries, PCM bilevel-event listings, and calibration curves for instrumentation downlink and meter link.

This is later-mission evidence and is **not** evidence for any LM-5 numerical value. It does, however, materially constrain the interpretation of the series: these volumes are telemetry/display measurement-calibration documents, not demonstrated engine-performance/acceptance-test reports.

Therefore the 12 Jun 1969 LM-5 volume remains worth recovering for CONTROL because it may identify how spacecraft measurements were converted/displayed, including any relevant DPS measurement channels. But its title alone no longer supports prioritizing it as the most likely source for a physical FTP thrust-to-force mapping.

## Direct FTP archival recovery path — refined 23 Sep 2026

The official Purdue University Archives catalog now gives an exact physical locator for the highest-priority Mayer item: **Neil A. Armstrong papers, MSA 5, Series 1, Box 58, Folder 4, Item 10**, “Effect of fixed throttle point thrust on the time from loss of radial guidance control to DPS throttle-down,” dated **5 Jun 1969**. The catalog's hierarchical identifier is **MSA 5, Series 1, Sub-Series 5, Sub-Group 4, File 6, Item 16**. This title/date independently matches the NASA/MSC H-2 lunar-descent reference to John P. Mayer memorandum **69-FMZ2-149**.

The two 8 Jul 1969 companion studies are physically adjacent in **MSA 5, Box 54, Folder 5, Items 14–15**: “Effects of DPS engine dispersions and LM weight on throttle-down time” and “Effects of known dispersions at PDI on throttle-down time.” Purdue's catalog explicitly gives Item 15 the hierarchical identifier **MSA 5, Series 1, Sub-Series 5, Sub-Group 4, File 2, Item 21**. No unshown identifier is inferred for Item 14.

This converts the FTP thread from a broad document search into a precise archival reproduction request. The underlying pages are still required before extracting any FTP thrust, dispersion, LM-weight, PDI, or throttle-down-time value.

## Priority correction

1. **Highest priority for FTP force:** request/recover Purdue MSA 5 **Box 58 / Folder 4 / Item 10** (Mayer 69-FMZ2-149, 5 Jun 1969).
2. In the same archival request, recover Purdue MSA 5 **Box 54 / Folder 5 / Items 14–15**, the two 8 Jul 1969 throttle-down dispersion studies.
3. Recover the LM-5 **Operational Calibration Curves Vol. II** from NARA Fort Worth, accession **72-A-1116 / RG 255 / agency box 71**, but inspect it first as a telemetry/display calibration source. Do not assume it contains engine physical-performance calibration.
4. Continue recovery of the underlying **LM-5 DPS Final Flight Evaluation** contractor report and engine acceptance/performance records for delivered thrust/Isp.
5. Keep **LMA790-3-LM §2.3.5 / p. 2.3-25 / Table 2.3-1**, exact PDI mass, and BET Volume II as blocked targets under their existing evidence rules.

## Additional archival locator

The official Purdue Armstrong finding aid also identifies **Apollo 11/LM-5 Post Mission Report, 5 Aug 1969**, in MSA 5 mission-planning files. Its content is not publicly exposed in the catalog, so no propulsion result is inferred. It is retained as a secondary LM-5-specific recovery target rather than treated as evidence for FTP calibration.
