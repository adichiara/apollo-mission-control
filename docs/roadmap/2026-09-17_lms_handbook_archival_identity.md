# Roadmap update — LMS handbook archival identity

Date: 2026-09-17

## Current state

Research notes 235, 236, 238, and 239–245 constrain both LMS compute-topology evidence and the next primary retrieval path.

The mature LMS is supported as a three-machine complex with one machine dedicated to onboard-guidance-computer simulation. The original two machine workloads remain unknown. Exact NASA/MSC accession keys are now known for the 1967 Grumman LMS instructor handbook and for a contemporaneous LMS Mission Effects Projector program review.

NASA/KSC GP-642 maps the exact Volume I title to GAEC `LMA 790-2-LMS`, dated Apr. 1, 1967. A public digitized Volume I title-match candidate is known, but its identity as the particular `67-14186` accession copy is not yet proven from the scan itself.

NASA/MSC corporate-author control separately identifies Grumman `LLR-400-329`, *Contract NAS9-1100, LMS Mission Effects Projector Program Review*, dated 06-07-67, accession `67-15490`. Its contents have not yet been recovered.

NASA NTRS `19680013122`, J. LaRussa's 1964 AIAA/NASA paper *The Infinity Image System in Visual Simulation*, directly describes a Farrand MEP as a non-programmed continuous color film-strip projector for earth-orbital imagery, with moving cloud/sunrise/sunset effects and two cassettes for different scales. This is direct architecture evidence for the Farrand MEP design lineage, but it predates the 1967 LMS program review and cannot be silently transferred to the exact deployed LMS configuration.

NASA TN D-7112 now directly closes the **generic mature LMS near-body visual technique**: common filmstrip → zoom optics → four screens → articulated optical probes, with altitude-dependent distortion and horizon mechanisms. It documents the evolution to Lunar Orbiter/Apollo-photograph film content and identifies real fidelity limits, including low vidicon illumination and optical-axis wander restricting usable zoom ratio to 3 rather than the design 10. This is system-level NASA evidence and removes the need to infer the mature LMS visual chain from the 1964 Farrand paper. It does not, however, identify every element as the formal MEP, establish vendor succession, or prove Apollo 13-period effectivity.

NASA NTRS `19690026999`, Aero Service's primary July 1969 *Final Report — LEM Mission Simulator Film Graphics* (`NAS 9-5981`), directly establishes a terrestrial/lunar film-graphics production path for MEP use, NASA/Grumman/Farrand coordination, and late-1967 Lunar-Orbiter-driven lunar-detail enhancement. It does not establish exact projector hardware configuration or vendor succession. NASA SP-4009 identifies a specific 30 Aug. 1967 Bethpage weekly report as the underlying record for the Farrand specification problem and proposed Itek study; retrieve that report before treating the sequence as primary-source closed.

## Metadata caution

Do not silently reconcile the `67-14186`/`67-14187` date discrepancy. The MSC main index and KSC GP-642 support 01-04-67; the MSC supplement gives 15-05-67.

## Gate

Bibliographic titles establish retrieval identity, not technical content. TN D-7112 establishes the mature generic LMS near-body visual chain, but not exact July 1967 subsystem nomenclature/vendor configuration or Apollo 13 effectivity. Do not assign the original two computers, exact MEP/EVDE boundary, causal domains, station products, cadence, acceptance tolerances, or a Farrand→Itek→Kollsman chain without opened linking technical/configuration records. Close model inputs only from adequate source evidence or under D-022 demonstrated irrelevance.
