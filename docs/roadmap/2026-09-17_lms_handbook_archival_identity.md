# Roadmap update — LMS handbook archival identity

Date: 2026-09-17

## Current state

Research notes 235, 238–246, and 302 constrain both LMS compute-topology evidence and the next primary retrieval path.

The mature LMS is supported as a three-machine complex with one machine dedicated to onboard-guidance-computer simulation. The original two machine workloads remain unknown. Exact NASA/MSC accession keys are known for the 1967 Grumman LMS instructor handbook and contemporaneous LMS Mission Effects Projector program review.

NASA/KSC GP-642 maps the exact Volume I title to GAEC `LMA 790-2-LMS`, dated Apr. 1, 1967. A public digitized Volume I title-match candidate is known, but its identity as the particular `67-14186` accession copy is not yet proven from the scan itself.

NASA/MSC corporate-author control identifies Grumman `LLR-400-329`, *Contract NAS9-1100, LMS Mission Effects Projector Program Review*, dated 06-07-67, accession `67-15490`. Its contents remain unrecovered.

NASA NTRS `19680013122` constrains the 1964 Farrand MEP design lineage. NASA TN D-7112 directly closes the generic mature LMS near-body visual technique: common filmstrip → zoom optics → four screens → articulated optical probes, with altitude-dependent distortion and horizon mechanisms. NASA NTRS `19690026999` establishes a later MEP film-content production path.

NASA SP-4009 now provides a tighter August 1967 procurement boundary, but remains synthesis evidence pending its cited primary report. It records that the Farrand-designed/built MEP did not meet established specifications and that Grumman sought a **study** from optomechanical firms. Kollsman was among seven interested firms; technical proposals are listed from Itek, Biorad, Link, and Conductron; Grumman judged Itek most technically acceptable and proposed an Itek letter contract, with NASA concurrence. Do not convert this study selection into an Itek hardware replacement claim, and do not connect later Kollsman `NAS9-8634` work to this action without a linking record.

## Gate

Do not assign original two-computer workloads, exact MEP/EVDE boundary, causal domains, station products, cadence, acceptance tolerances, an Itek hardware replacement, or a Farrand→Itek→Kollsman succession without adequate evidence.
