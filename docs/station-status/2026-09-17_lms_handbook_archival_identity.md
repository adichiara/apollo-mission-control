A public digitized Volume I candidate is known, but its scan-level accession/revision/site identity remains unverified.

NASA/MSC corporate-author control identifies Grumman `LLR-400-329`, *Contract NAS9-1100, LMS Mission Effects Projector Program Review*, dated 06-07-67, MSC accession `67-15490`. This is an exact primary retrieval target, not yet technical evidence about the July 1967 LMS MEP implementation.

NASA NTRS `19680013122` provides direct contemporaneous technical evidence for a 1964 Farrand MEP architecture: a non-programmed continuous color film-strip projector for earth-orbital views, including moving cloud cover and sunrise/sunset effects, using two film cassettes for different scales. These details constrain the Farrand design lineage but are not promoted as the exact deployed MSC/KSC LMS configuration.

NASA TN D-7112 now directly establishes the mature LMS near-body visual chain at system level: a common filmstrip through zoom optics to four screens, articulated optical probes, altitude-dependent spherical-distortion optics and horizon generation, and lunar film content evolving through Lunar Orbiter and Apollo photography. It also records material fidelity limitations, including reduced vidicon illumination and optical-axis wander that restricted usable zoom ratio to 3 rather than the design 10. This closes the generic LMS visual technique without proving exact July 1967 MEP/EVDE nomenclature, vendor ownership, or Apollo 13-period configuration.

NASA NTRS `19690026999` provides direct contractor technical evidence for the later MEP content pipeline: Aero Service's LMS film-graphics work under `NAS 9-5981` included terrestrial/lunar graphics, coordination with NASA/Grumman/Farrand, and a 30 Nov. 1967 change authorization requiring Lunar-Orbiter-based enhancement techniques applicable to MEP film production. This closes a visual-content-production relationship but not exact deployed projector hardware, computer ownership, vendor succession, or station routing.
