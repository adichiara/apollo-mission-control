Historical station maturity grades remain unchanged.

Research note 233 establishes from the primary RG 255 E.155B1 inventory description that `NAS9-8634` was for **construction of LM simulators at both MSC and KSC**. This is a simulator-site/project fact, not a Mission Control station fact. File-level subsystem scope remains unrecovered from public primary-source discovery.

Research note 242 further constrains the E.155B lead: E.155B is an LLTV reference-file series that also contains some LMS MEP/EVDE material, whereas E.155B1 is the dedicated LMS project-file series. Archival co-location of MEP/EVDE material with LLTV/LLRV records is not evidence of a Mission Control interface or technical relationship.

Research note 243 establishes from primary NASA 1966 program records that Grumman's converted LEM development contract already required **two mission simulators** by 15 February 1966, before the 1968–1970 E.155B1 Kollsman / `NAS9-8634` project-file span. Accordingly, the later inventory phrase “construction of LM simulators at both MSC and KSC” must not be promoted into a claim that `NAS9-8634` originated the LMS requirement or created the simulator/Mission Control relationship. Its implementation meaning remains a file-level retrieval question.

No Mission Control display, routing, telemetry, controller workflow, Section 7 output mapping, or Apollo 13 LMS effectivity was recovered. Do not promote station claims from the contract-series description, the E.155B archival neighborhood, chronology alone, the word “construction,” or negative search results.

Next station-relevant promotion requires direct E.155B1 or E.155B MEP/EVDE evidence that explicitly identifies simulator outputs, interfaces, site/configuration, or downstream routing.
